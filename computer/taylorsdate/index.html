On a hot summer day, at the middle of nowhere, inside a boring air-conditioned fast food restaurant branded as 'Gloogs', Taylor is sat all alone on a reserved table waiting for his lunch. As a bead of sweat runs by his cheek, he sighs at the sight of Toby and Sophie unfortunately hanging out together in a table far away. They audibly laughed, smiled, made a slight mess, typical couple things. Our lovesick Taylor could not help but just stare at them, specifically Toby, as he melted more onto the steel table. He'd spend time imagining him frolicking with Toby in grassy fields covered by pink and gold flowers, as the sun sets behind them and the wind blows with a soothing slow breeze. It's been years, he could not get over his face, his mutual wimpy and pathetic personality. He knew it would never work, yet he couldn't get over it ever since the day Sophie held Toby's hands for the first time.

"Yo." A familiar voice came by him, "Still staring at Toby?"

"Huh-" Taylor quickly moved from the table, "Hey!-" He stuttered, the consequence of laying down on the table apparent on his weirdly shaped sticky fur.

His friend Jessica, dressed in worker's attire, gently placed his tray of food on the table before reaching out to her back-pocket to get her small handheld fan. She turned it on and held it to his face without saying a single word, Taylor's eyes slightly twitched from the refreshing breeze.

Jessica put the fan back to her pocket, "You already know, dude. It's a miracle he hasn't seen you like that, or... even know you feel that way to him." She shrugged, shaking her head with closed eyes, "I can't hang around for a chat, sorry, gotta get back to work. See ya..."

"Um, sure!" He slightly waved back before turning back to his lunch.

"Uh..." He was perplexed soon after. Toby and Sophie have already left.

As Jessica continued her shift she couldn't help but notice how bothered Taylor looked. He's felt like this for a long time, she tried to help him with it, but all attempts went nowhere. This was a problem he needed to resolve for himself, and she needed something, just something that would push him to move on from malicious feelings such as love. She thought back to her teenage years, to times when she felt just like him about a cute classmate, to what she would do each weekend... There it was, lunch break.

The sun outside was unforgiving, arguably deadly, yet one particular unemployed rascal spent their time in the park lighting up ants with a magnifying glass while snickering about it.

Jessica pinched her face with a hand, shaking their head, "Having fun?"

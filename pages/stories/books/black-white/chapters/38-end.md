The forgotten plains quietly rustle as soft gusts of wind cut through the ashy white grass. Snowflakes fell from the top of the blinding white sky, slowly and hesitantly. Ferd opened his eyes slowly, squinting at the sight of a distant familiar figure before him.

"*Hey, Ferd...*"

He could barely hear the words she said. Ferd reached his right hand out to her, free of the gauntlet at last, his steps felt like his first.

And then he awoke, back in the familiar pine forests of Onder-5, back in his decorated little storage container where he had spent most of his service in. His scrappy home was the same as it always was, untouched and dusty since his sudden departure. He looked back and forth, wide awake. He looked back at his right arm again, he was finally free of the gauntlet, though his arm was scarred and black.

Ferd didn't hesitate to swiftly put on his old Disobedient service clothes and drink some water. It was eerily quiet for an evening. There was a chilling lack of music, work, humanity in general. The only noise that accompanied Ferd was just the wind and the sound of leaves rustling.

"Hello?" He stuck his head out of the window and asked the silence, "Anyone there?"

But there was no answer.

He stepped outside to check for any signs of life. No one was around. He stepped into the main scrappy building where they held all the meetings, there was only one message pinned on the main board, lousily taped together with pages from various newspapers.

"The war is over." Was written in bold text. Ferd squinted his eyes to look at the dates, years 2765 to 2769 were the most prominent. He felt his heart sink. "The Citadel's ideology collapsed, and with it the Disobedient's ruthless hunger for war. Sweet home waits for us, people. We are finally free."

He took a step back in shock. He looked around, ran his finger through a seat, everything was dusty and unattended for at least a few years. He took a bunch of newspapers from the batch behind the board, all from various dates, and began reading as he stepped out of the building to look for any more answers.

"Year 2765, the end of La Reina's wrath. The devastating monochrome era is declared to be over, major powers of the universe agree to a truce and rebuild from the catastrophes that occurred..."

Ferd was sat in the makeshift cafeteria all by himself, reading as he sipped from his coffee mug that brought back a whole bunch of nostalgia.

"Year 2766, the Citadel and the New Babylon is trialed over the use of reality-destroying weapons. The New Babylon claims their partnership was never meant to bring terror; peace, safety, and prosperity to all worlds was their main goal, not destruction..."

Ferd walked through the forest, often raising his head from the newspaper to see if he was going the right direction.

"Year 2767. Time travel has been attained by the Citadel's hidden branch, the final step of technology. Several black operations have quickly spread out this technology for all the powers to use like wildfire. May our loved ones be safe..."

Ferd made a surprised face reading that, his head slightly recoiling. Looking out at the gray surroundings, some of the questions he had running in his mind may have been already answered just by that headline. Despite that, he kept walking and reading.

"Year 2768. Several powers of the universe have fallen due to the irresponsible use of time travel. Powers beyond comprehension, likely from unknown places of space or different universes, have completely decimated the core systems of the Disobedient and Citadel. The Disobedient's 'Centuries Old Man' passed away from a heart attack..."

Ferd made an unsurprised shrugging expression reading that. Thankfully, it didn't seem like everything turned into a complete disaster, unless they're already under control of some bizarre alien power. He wouldn't know, so he kept reading.

"Year 2769. Survivors, soldiers, anyone still in service stranded on planets formerly under control of powers no longer around are recalled back to new homes by the New Babylon through extensive extraction operations..."

Finally, Ferd's face turns away from the newspaper. He made his way back to the cliff where he and Ena spent hours each day, showering each other with compliments and love. The place where they used to sit was adored with lit candles, wilted flowers both natural and made of plastic, printed memories carefully placed in frames, and most importantly, Ena's personal guitar and his round black glasses. Ferd placed the newspapers beside him and dropped to his knees with so many thoughts running through his mind. Various long memories and messages were written by most members of the former chaotic group he was once a part of on frail but carefully attached pieces of paper.

"...I'll really miss ya man, I'll truly do." -Isaac Sir Kablooey.
"...One of a kind, one of a kind." -Vladimir Koshinov.

But two caught his eyes the most.

"...I hope you'll forgive me someday, Ferd... I'm sorry I wasn't there, that I never refused my forced mission. In our times when we were together, I truly loved spending time with you, and if I could I would do anything to turn back time to hear your voice again." -Houston March.
"...Ferdi, please come back, we miss you. You would never believe what's happened since you disappeared. There's so much I'd love to talk to you about... I love you, Ferd. I truly love you." -Ally Parks.

A picture from an old adventure back on Zuma II stood by the two messages left by Houston and Ally. Where Ally was smiling with her whole heart out, throwing a peace sign, where Houston was so chilled out with a soft smile taking the selfie, and where he, Ferd, stood at the back with a bit of a forced smile.

He picked up the picture to take a closer look and reflect. Soft tears from his eyes fell onto the memory, slightly staining the edge. He spent so little time with them, with the people that mattered to him the most. The waves of the ocean kept crashing against the cliff as he slowly let himself cry. He was loved, remembered, throughout the whole four years of absence. He noticed even more of his memories placed besides his glasses, and he'd continue to mourn the time he lost.

Footsteps were heard from behind, walking on the dry leaves leaving a repeating crunching noise.

"Uh-" Ferd took a moment to recollect his train of thought, rubbing his eyes, "Who is..."

"Don't worry, I am not here to crash your moment..." A tall woman gently walked to his shrine, her hands formally tucked behind her back. "Bringing back the fire to the candles is all I am here to do..."

And that's when her eyes made contact with Ferd.

"...Who are you?" Ferd asked, confused, with a groggy voice.

"My name is Hazy, I don't believe we have ever met before." She crouched down, offering her hand to him. "You must be..."

"...Ferd, yes." He sniffled, taking her hand, "You're one of the Inka, right?"

"That's right." She gently nodded.

"What are you doing here?" He asked.

"I come to this shrine every day, every week. Bringing flowers, bringing candles for both of you..." She gently placed down a fresh bouquet of black and white daisies, along with scented candles that she lit with the tip of her finger, "Your partner, who you have known as Ena Lone was once my beloved sister. It's important for me to stand here, cherish the memory of your chaotic adventures, and move on with my day. The universe doesn't feel the same with your absence, it truly does not."

Ferd had nothing to say, the sinking feeling in his chest only continued as he looked at his memorabilia.

"Such is death. Memento mori." She stood besides him, quiet to honor the memory.

The wind blew gently, the salt in the air slightly bothering their eyes.

After a minute, Ferd turned to her face and asked, "...Am I dead?"

"You looked at death's face and refused its offer." She smiled, "I hope you know that's impressive, not many tend to do that."

"I've avoided death multiple times, I did..." He looked at his scarred arm, where the once obtrusive golden gauntlet was once embedded.

"Tell me..." She looked at the ocean's distant horizon, "What do you plan on doing with your second chance?"

"I don't know, I'm lost." He looked down, "I was brought back into a world I don't belong to anymore."

"Oh, silly. Seeking guidance as usual, hm?"

"Not that, I just..."

Hazy slowly pointed at the sky, interrupting his speech. 

"Fata viam invenient."

He simply tilted his head in confusion, trying to quietly figure out what she means by that.

"Everything always comes full circle. What do you remember from the last time an Inka talked to you here?" Her finger was now pointing at him.

"You want me to steal a ship again and fly back to the Babylon?"

Hazy turned around, and with a smile, "They need you more than ever, Ferd."

In a blink she was already gone. Ferd was left alone once more, but this time he knew what to do. He took his own glasses from the shrine and put them back on his forehead.

Fate finds its way.

Through the stars on yet another stolen Disobedient ship he soared.

Fate finds its way.

Through the New Babylon's moon, he landed and bypassed an identity check that never did its job right.

Fate finds its way.

He rushed to catch a transporter to Saint Kalina, one of the Babylon's many capital cities. And then he rushed through the streets, through the never-ending crowds of people, desperately looking for any direction to the city's known museum. Without paying any attention to his close surroundings, he slammed right into a stranger. Their shopping bags and flowers went flying into the air. Ferd winced, he leapt to catch the paper bags and flowers with both of his hands like an experienced jester, and with it he swiftly mended his fault of whoever he bumped into.

"I'm so sorry about that!!!" He frantically gave their stuff back to them, "I'm in a rush! You know, no big deal-"

In the next moment, the stranger hugged them tightly.

"You... You came back." They spoke, trying their hardest to hold back tears.

"H-Houston?" He stuttered, recognizing their voice.

"Yes! You clumsy idiot!" They pushed away from the hug, with teary eyes and pure joy in their face just to see him, "How many more times are you gonna crash into me like that, huh?!"

"Houston..." He went in for the hug again, this time from his side, "Oh god, I've missed you, I've missed you a lot." He let his feelings flourish.

"Tell me about it..." They squeezed him, blushing, sharing the same feelings. "You look absolutely terrible! I need to get you fixed up asap, no time to waste."

Before Ferd could even process everything that happened, Houston already grabbed his hand and tugged him along to the closest clothing store.

Before Ferd could even process everything that happened there, Houston already grabbed their hand again and tugged him out of the clothing store. Ferd was now dressed in a sleek familiar fit handpicked by Houston themself, cleaned thoroughly. Ferd was surprised at Houston's acting, they never were this frantic and energetic. Everything was happening way too fast.

"Oh, this will be the happiest day of her life." Houston couldn't hold back their joy.

"Who?" Ferd raised an eyebrow, holding all the bags and flowers they were previously carrying.

"Just wait and see. Get excited already! I can almost see a smile on your face."

Much to Ferd's curiosity, he and Houston ignored the museum completely, heading to a different place instead.

"I can see you starin' there, don't. Imma catch you up on everything later."

They were like a bullet, Ferd could barely catch up. "Huh, o-okay."

A grand hospital building stood in front of Ferd and Houston, he had many questions that he couldn't say out loud, not yet. Houston blitzed through the whole check-in process, making sure they came at the right hours that accepted visitors like Ferd.

"Can I start asking questions-"

"No."

Houston would always shoot down any attempt at asking, especially in the long elevator ride all the way to the 14th floor. Ferd had a worried feeling in his chest, especially when Houston got pretty quiet in the hallway. They stopped by a door.

"Ferd." They looked at him, "Stay here, I don't want her to combust."

"Combust?" He was anxious about the whole situation.

"You know what I mean." They took the bags and flowers from his hands before going into the room without another word.

Ferd was left blank right outside, he could hear muffled voices coming from inside, mostly Houston's. It didn't take long before Houston came back to Ferd, they really couldn't hold their excitement. Houston gave him their own bouquet of black and white flowers they had into his hands, and then brought him into the room.

What he finally figured out...

The patient they were visiting was Ally, staring out the window stuck in a hospital bed. She gracefully looked back at Houston, and there she saw him again after four long years. They were both stunned, without a word to say.

"You..." Her eyes got wet, "Houston..." She began crying.

Ferd slowly walked to her, feeling just as shocked. The moment he got close enough Ally held onto his arm dragged him onto the bed with her.

"You... You actually..." Her voice was broken, interrupted with many sniffles, "You came back..." Her face was completely buried on his arm.

He responded with a tight hug.

"Everything was just so hard... So dark since you left..." Her arms wrapped him. "You're back..!"

As Ferd and Ally had their emotional moment, Houston was busy unpacking food they had prepared for Ally before.

"Where have you been you jerk!" Ally pulled away from the hug, "Where have you been?!" She weakly punched at his chest, her fingertips oozing a black fluid.

"I'm sorry!" His voice hitched, "I would've... I should've..."

"Ally." Houston spoke up, bringing attention to them, "I got you a sandwich. Hungry?"

"...Very." 

She reached out to nab it and then began munching on it as if her life depended on it. It seemed to cool her down.

"I'm not sharing." She noticed him looking at her sandwich. It was his old favorite type, a turkey club sandwich.

Houston was, and has been nervous about it, tugging at the collar of their shirt.

"Yeah that was pretty deli thanks Houst." She was still chewing, but she tossed the disposable paper packaging back at their hand. She seemed better, but the marks from tears were still visible. Ferd was still sat on the bed, awkwardly.

"I'm not done with you." She grabbed onto his arm every time he moved, even if he wasn't willing to move away. "I have, so much to talk to you about." She sniffled.

Houston took a deep breath, thinking, "*Not how I expected my day to go, not at all.*"

Ferd finally worked up the courage to ask, "What happened while I was gone?" With a worried tone.

"Not answering until you tell your part." Ally was a bit grumpy.

"Yeah, where the hell have you been?" Houston joined in.

Ferd took a moment to think, and in the next second...

He found himself in the forgotten plains again, along with the unknown figure.

"*Hey, Ferd...*"

He could barely make out the words she said, but he was willing to listen instead of reaching out.

"*It's okay to look ahead...*"

As the fragmented memory came together, he managed to tell everything that occurred to him since the last time he saw Ally. The whole tale of Black & White he was told by the Inka, his final confrontation with La Reina, and then... the unexplained departure of four long years, to which he himself didn't know the answer to.

"*I've lived a beautiful life, one of my personal favorites of all...*"

Suddenly, he woke up from the daydream. Houston and Ally were staring at him.

"Oh, this would fascinate... Can I talk about that? You cool with it?" Houston said.

"Of course."

"Yeah, Rose, that guy. Since the death of his 'beloved' creation he hasn't been taking things well. Sure wonder what he's up to." They thought.

"Baseball probably got stuck in time during the whole time travel fiasco." Ally added.

"Ally..." Ferd didn't really seem to care about such things at the moment, "I want to know what happened to you."

Ally uncovered her mechanical leg from the sheets. "They stole my leg, can't have shit in the Babylon."

"Because of a faulty heart transplant, cancer has developed inside of her." They knew she wouldn't give a proper answer.

Ferd's heart sank at the mention of cancer.

"Me and Ally both had a similar case of inheriting an Inka's heart, though mine was done in an advanced surgery, and hers was done in a frantic last-resort sort of way..." Houston gestured with their hands as they spoke, "Never knew such thing could even happen, I was sure it would be compatible no matter what."

"Thank god for them to come by and reconnect after you vanished. I've been living a cool life here since then, the rich still infuriate me with their fancy rituals." Ally sipped on a juice box.

"She has been kind of infecting me with *her* rituals."

"Houst you are a cool sibling I like a lot."

"*And she's not saying that because I keep making her sandwiches!*" They quietly added.

It got quiet after.

"Ferd, don't be sad about me having that." She noticed him having a lot of heavy thoughts.

"I-It's fine." He struggled to speak.

"Noticed you finally got rid of that crazy gauntlet, must be a relief."

"Yeah..."

Ally stretched her left arm right besides his blackened right arm. "Look at that, both completely screwed up, we're matching!"

He couldn't help but smile.

The noon in the New Babylon quickly turned into an evening, and then night time fell upon the city. Ferd, Houston, and Ally have swiftly reconnected, sharing stories and general talks about whatever drifted through their minds. Soon, Houston and Ferd had to leave the building however, and outside Ferd could only think about her.

"She'll be fine, right?" He asked.

"She's been fighting it for years. She's strong, Ferd, the strongest there ever is. If anything I bet death would cower in fear from her presence alone."

"You two have gotten along so much, haven't you?"

"She's family to me."

"Never heard you mention your family before, actually."

"I used to be alone, let's not dwell too much into it. You'll be stayin' over at my place, sound cool?"

"For sure..." He looked back at the hospital building.

Suddenly, he found himself back in the forgotten plains, this time however he saw the figure clearly.

"Ena..."

"*Ferd...*"

"*It's okay to look ahead.*"

"*I've lived a beautiful life, one of my personal favorites of all...*"

Sudden memories of their past relationship wash up. The high school years, the rough adulthood, the seven years of Disobedient service. All the love, all the chaos, all the adventures.

"*But you have yet to live your desired normal life, so I say...*"

Sudden feelings from the deepest parts of their heart wash up. A soul within, wishing for a peaceful life, full of love to give and receive.

"*Reach out to those you love the most, and make every minute of it count!*"

And at last, the memories of Ally, even before he had seen her as the Ally she has been since the four years ago. Two friends since childhood with complicated feelings for each other that were never expressed. Ena knew this, and with a smirk, she pushed the hesitant Ferd out of the forgotten plains to pursue what truly matters.

"Actually, Houston." He suddenly stopped, giving them the bag they were just carrying.

"Hm?" They turned back, confused.

"I'll see you tomorrow, I'll be there." He started walking backwards "No more regrets."

Houston scoffed at him as he ran, "...Reckless idiot."

In the cold and dark hospital room on the fourteenth floor, Ferd had managed to climb to the window and barge his way inside.
